# Sector modules

Use with `core.md` and the **one** market file. Skip sections that do not match the company.

## Banks

Do **not** use CFO/FCF like a non-financial.

Always: P/B vs sustainable ROE vs COE; NIM vs cost of funds / deposit beta; CASA; stage-2 / group-2 / classified; NPL; coverage/LLR; credit cost / NCO; CET1 or CAR; LDR; loan vs deposit growth; CRE / construction / bond concentration; related-party lending; collateral mix; profit quality from bancassurance, securities, reversals, trading.

Through-cycle ROE, not this year. A cheap P/E on peak credit-cost trough is a trap.

Market extras live in `markets/*.md` (VN recap/policy lending; US AOCI/HTM; AU housing/APRA).

## Tech, outsourcing, software

Bookings and conversion to revenue **and cash**; revenue/employee; headcount; utilization; attrition; segment margins; receivables; product/IP vs labor mix; whether AI raises *their* take-rate or lets the customer cut price; commoditization; one-market or one-customer dependence.

Software: FCF after SBC, net retention. Rule of 40 is marketing. VN IT services (FPT/CMG) = outsourcing/SI/telecom, **not** an AI product — only open `overlays/ai-semis.md` if the thesis is actually silicon/hyperscaler capex.

## Semis / foundry / equipment / hyperscaler AI capex

Stop. Read `overlays/ai-semis.md` before valuation. Never use LTM peak.

## Steel, chemicals, heavy industry

Mid-cycle earnings, never peak: capacity and utilization, cost curve, input vs selling prices and spreads, capex, debt, FCF after new projects ramp, trade defense, real construction demand, oversupply.

## Consumer / retail

Share and brand; distribution; volume vs price; GPM; ad spend; working capital; pricing power without losing customers; cash and dividends; mature-market, private-label, and online substitution. Do not treat a doubled recovery year as trough EPS.

## Property / REITs

Per-project legal (`VN`); presales and cash actually collected; handover; NAV that can become cash; debt/bonds; capitalized interest; contractor payables; related parties; land-price dependence; dilution. REITs: LTV, refi wall, occupancy, rent vs inflation. High yield can still be a trap. Default high bar; most developers fail.

## Brokers

Mix of brokerage, margin, prop; prop-book quality; cost of capital; equity; liquidity cycle; earnings **normalized across cycles**; dilution to raise capital. Never value one boom-trading quarter.
